Phishing SCENARIO_01

Urgent Email Attack

The attacker impersonates your IT department and creates panic to make you act without thinking.

Live simulation — what you would see in your inbox
inbox — mail client simulation
From: IT-Security@c0mpany-helpdesk.net
To: you@yourcompany.com
Date: Today, 09:14 AM
⚠ URGENT: Suspicious login detected — verify your account within 2 hours

Dear Employee,

Our automated security systems have detected unusual login activity from an unrecognised device on your account. To protect company data, your account access has been temporarily restricted.

You must verify your identity within 2 hours or your account will be permanently suspended and the incident will be escalated to your manager.

Click the button below to verify now and restore access:

🔒 Verify My Account Now

If you did not trigger this alert, contact IT immediately at: it-support@c0mpany-helpdesk.net
© 2026 Company IT Security Team | Do not reply to this email

Identified red flags
⚠ 5 red flags in this email
1
Fake domain with character substitution "c0mpany-helpdesk.net" — the letter O is replaced with zero. This is a homoglyph attack. Always read email domains character by character.
2
Extreme urgency — "2 hours" Artificial time pressure is the #1 social engineering tactic. It stops you from thinking rationally and checking with your real IT team.
3
Threat of severe consequences "Permanently suspended" and "escalated to your manager" trigger fear and shame — powerful psychological levers attackers exploit.
4
Generic greeting Real company IT departments know your name. "Dear Employee" is a clear sign this email was sent in bulk to many targets.
5
Reply-to matches the fake domain The "contact IT" address also uses the fake domain. Attackers control both sides of the communication.
How to respond correctly
✓ Correct actions
  • 1Do NOT click any links — hover over them first to see the real URL
  • 2Check the sender's full email address character by character
  • 3Contact your real IT team via phone or official Slack/Teams channel
  • 4Report the email as phishing using your email client's report button
  • 5Alert your colleagues — if you received it, others likely did too
