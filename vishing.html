Voice Phishing SCENARIO_02

Vishing (Voice Phishing)

Vishing uses phone calls to manipulate victims. The attacker impersonates trusted authority figures to extract information verbally.

Live simulation — a call you might receive at work
incoming call — phone simulation
INCOMING CALL
+44 0800 123 456
IT Helpdesk — Internal
ATTACKER
"Hello, this is James from the IT helpdesk. Am I speaking with a member of the finance team?"
YOU
"Yes, that's me. How can I help?"
ATTACKER
"We've detected a critical security breach affecting finance accounts. I'm calling affected users personally to resolve this urgently before it escalates. I just need to confirm your employee ID and reset your credentials remotely — it'll only take two minutes."
YOU
"Oh no, that sounds serious. My employee ID is... EM-2047..."
ATTACKER
"Perfect, thank you. Now I need your current password so I can migrate the account safely. Don't worry — this line is encrypted and I'm legally required to keep all details confidential."
⚠ STOP — this is where the attack succeeds if you continue
Identified red flags
⚠ 5 red flags in this call
1
Unsolicited inbound call Real IT helpdesks don't call you about problems you didn't report. If there's truly a breach, IT uses verified internal channels first.
2
Asking for your password verbally No legitimate IT professional ever asks for your password. Password resets are done through verified self-service portals — never over a phone call.
3
Requesting your employee ID Your employee ID alone is enough for an attacker to impersonate you in further attacks. Never confirm internal identifiers to unverified callers.
4
False authority and urgency "Critical breach", "legally required" — these phrases manufacture authority and fear to stop you verifying who you're actually speaking to.
5
Caller ID can be spoofed The display showing "IT Helpdesk — Internal" means nothing. Phone caller ID is trivially easy to fake using widely available tools.
How to respond correctly
✓ Correct actions during this call
  • 1Politely say: "I need to verify your identity before continuing"
  • 2Ask for their employee number and department, then hang up
  • 3Call IT back on the official number from your company directory
  • 4Never give your password to anyone — even real IT doesn't need it
  • 5Report the call to your security team immediately
